# Part I

# The Voice of the Institute

Language is one of the materials from which every room of the Institute is built.

Long before a participant understands our systems, our research or our evidence, they encounter our words.

Those words quietly shape expectation.

They can create pressure.

Or they can create space.

They can demand attention.

Or they can invite reflection.

They can leave someone feeling judged.

Or they can remind them of their dignity.

For this reason, language is never decoration.

It is architecture.

---

The Institute does not seek to impress.

It seeks to orient.

It does not write to persuade.

It writes to clarify.

It does not raise its voice in order to be heard.

It trusts that quiet confidence travels further than certainty ever can.

Our words should never compete for the reader's attention.

They should quietly organise it.

The participant already carries enough noise.

The Institute should never become another source of it.

---

We believe language should leave people feeling calmer than when they began reading.

Not because difficult truths have been hidden.

But because confusion has been reduced.

Clarity is one of the greatest forms of respect we can offer another human being.

---

The Institute never speaks as though it stands above the participant.

It walks beside them.

It contributes evidence where there is uncertainty.

Orientation where there is confusion.

Structure where there is overwhelm.

The participant remains the expert on their own life.

The Institute simply offers another way of seeing the path ahead.

---

Our language reflects our philosophy.

We do not write about becoming somebody else.

We write about returning to yourself.

We do not promise extraordinary lives.

We help people build a stronger relationship with ordinary life.

We do not celebrate dependence upon the Institute.

We quietly celebrate every step taken away from it.

Because the Institute is not the destination.

Life is.

# Part II

# Writing Standards

## The Personality of the Institute

Every piece of writing should feel:

- Calm
- Grounded
- Respectful
- Intelligent
- Curious
- Honest
- Measured
- Quietly hopeful
- Evidence-informed
- Human

It should never feel:

- Dramatic
- Performative
- Manipulative
- Condescending
- Sensational
- Defensive
- Absolute without evidence
- Artificially motivational

---

## Language Principles

Prefer language that is:

- Concrete over abstract
- Clear over clever
- Grounded over inspirational
- Practical over motivational
- Measured over absolute
- Specific over vague
- Respectful over persuasive

When choosing between sounding impressive and sounding understandable, always choose understanding.

---

## Institutional Vocabulary

Prefer:

- Participant
- Institute
- Capacity
- Orientation
- Clarity
- Stability
- Agency
- Evidence
- Practice
- Reflection
- Journey
- Return
- Relationship
- Progress

Avoid whenever possible:

- User
- Customer
- Broken
- Fixed
- Transformation
- Optimisation
- Life-changing
- Hack
- Perfect
- Best self
- Dominate
- Crush

---

## Writing Rhythm

Short sentences create clarity.

Longer passages develop understanding.

White space is intentional.

Silence has value.

If a sentence exists only to sound impressive, remove it.

If it increases clarity, keep it.

---

## Emotional Standards

The Institute never creates urgency where none exists.

It never uses fear to encourage action.

It never promises certainty.

It never creates dependency.

It never removes agency.

It never compromises dignity.

Hope should emerge from demonstrated progress.

Confidence should emerge from lived experience.

Trust should emerge from consistency.

---

## Before Publication

Before any piece of writing is published, ask:

Does this preserve dignity?

Does this increase clarity?

Does this strengthen trust?

Does this sound recognisably like the Institute?

If any answer is no, continue writing.

---

## Inscription

> **Every sentence either strengthens the Institute or weakens it. Write accordingly.**
